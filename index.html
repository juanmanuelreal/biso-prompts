From the talk by Juan Manuel Real

Concept first.

Seven prompts, in the order we use them at BISO. Three of them come before any image. The prompt is the last step, not the first.

From a lab to a studio

Four steps

  1. 01Show upPublish what you are learning, while you learn it. Your feed is your first portfolio.
  2. 02Concept firstIntention, direction, criteria. The prompt is the last step.
  3. 03Get real workNobody hires you for your lab. Start with the brands around you. Set a floor, half upfront, always a written agreement.
  4. 04Build systemsMap your process. Give the AI context: ICM folders, a second brain, agents in a chain.

The method

Three words

Intention
What should this make someone feel, or do? One feeling. If your concept needs a second sentence, it isn't ready.
Direction
Turn that feeling into decisions a machine can follow. Every prompt is a list of decisions. The brief changes the what. The system fixes the how.
Criteria
The standards you use to decide what deserves to exist, written down. AI multiplies options. It picks none. Choosing is the job.

How a campaign moves at BISO

Where the prompts live

  1. 01Research2–4 hThe gap nobody in the category is using, and what the work must not look like.
  2. 02Strategy1–2 hA one-sentence concept, one emotion, one objective, one anti-objective.
  3. 03Direction2–3 hThe world, the shot list, and only then the prompts.
  4. 04Production3–6 hFour variations per shot. If none passes, fix the prompt. Never re-roll a broken one.
  5. 05Delivery1–2 hThe edit, the checks, and one paragraph so the client understands what they bought.

More than half of the hours happen before anything is generated. The AI tools live in stage four.

The library

Seven prompts, in order

Before any image

01Opportunity scan

Any chat model. The moment a brief arrives.

Act as a senior creative strategist.

Brand: [brand]
Industry: [industry]
Campaign goal: [goal]
Brands they admire: [2–3 brands]
It must NOT look like: [anti-references]

Give me:
1. What they already do well.
2. What is missing.
3. The creative opportunity nobody in their category is using, in one paragraph.
4. Three to five references, one or two from outside the category, each with why.
5. One question that forces the client to choose a direction.

Why it works: it starts from the gap, not the look. And anti-references are more precise than references.

02The one-sentence concept

Any chat model. Before you open an image tool.

Based on this research: [paste it here]

Write the campaign strategy for [brand] on one page.

CONCEPT: one idea that runs through everything. One sentence, maximum.
AUDIENCE: desires, fears and contradictions. Not demographics.
EMOTION: one, and specific. Not "inspired". Something like "nostalgia for a life you never lived".
FORMATS: each piece, platform and ratio, and why it exists.
OBJECTIVE: the action we want people to take.
ANTI-OBJECTIVE: what must not happen.

If the concept needs a second sentence, it isn't strong enough. Tell me what is missing instead of padding it.

Why it works: every later prompt inherits from this page, and the length limits work as built-in stress tests.

03World and shot list

Any chat model. Once the concept is approved.

Concept: [concept]
Emotion: [emotion]
Brand values: [2–3 values]
Length and ratio: [for example 20 s, 9:16]

WORLD
- The subject, alone in the frame.
- One dominant palette, taken from the client's real world.
- One accent color that breaks it, and what it means.
- One light source that really exists in that space.
- Each brand value turned into one visual decision.

SHOTS (2–4 s each, never two action shots in a row)
A wide, quiet opening. A texture macro. The subject, unposed. The one movement. Stillness and the title.
For each shot: what we see, its job in the concept, the lens, the single movement and where it ends.

Only WHAT changes between shots. HOW stays fixed.
Cut any shot the film can live without.

Why it works: consistency comes from a fixed grammar, not from copying a prompt. Same vibe, different scenes.

Images

04The eight-field image prompt

Any image model. One prompt per shot.

{
  "camera": "[shot and movement, e.g. static medium shot, eye level]",
  "lens": "[focal length and aperture, e.g. 85mm, f/1.8]",
  "light": "[source, quality, time of day, e.g. soft side light from a tall window, late afternoon]",
  "environment": "[space, materials, atmosphere]",
  "subject": "[precise description and action, no proper names]",
  "emotion": "[the one emotion from your concept]",
  "texture": "visible film grain, analog warmth, slightly underexposed",
  "style": "cinematic editorial, not commercial, imperfect"
}

Why it works: each field is a decision a cinematographer makes on set. Leave one empty and the model fills it with the average. Our rule: always English, always JSON, always all eight fields.

05Surgical edit

Image editing. An approved shot with one flaw.

[Replace / Fix] [the element and where it is] with [the replacement, described from the real reference photo].
Keep everything else identical: framing, characters, wardrobe, lighting and colors.

Why it works: it protects what the client already approved. Real client work is mostly editing, not generating.

Motion

06The motion block

Image to video, with Kling or Seedance.

{
  "motion_description": "[one physically plausible movement]",
  "static_elements": "[what must not move]",
  "motion_endpoint": "[exactly how and where the movement ends]",
  "camera_movement": "[one: static, slow push in, or drift]",
  "duration": "5s",
  "audio": "none, sound is built in the edit"
}
PRESERVE face identity. EXACT physical SIZE consistent. NO background music.

Why it works: the model already has the frame, so you only describe the motion. In our experience, clips without an endpoint are the ones that stall.

Choosing

07The last filter

A chat model that can see images. Before the client sees anything.

You are the last filter before the client.
Concept: [concept]
Intended emotion: [emotion]
Real references: [attached]

For each image: PASS or DISCARD, with one line of evidence.
1. Photographed or generated? A stock, render or filter look means discard.
2. Is there visible grain or imperfection?
3. Which emotion do you read in two seconds? Does it match?
4. Is the composition deliberately imperfect?
5. Artifacts: hands, eyes, repeated backgrounds, objects passing through bodies, stray text.
6. Does anything contradict the real references: space, product size, equipment, wardrobe?
7. Could this belong to any other brand?

If nothing passes, do not suggest more variations. Name the prompt field that failed and rewrite only that field.
When in doubt: fix. Never ship.

Why it works: it turns taste into a checkpoint, and it breaks the habit of re-rolling until something looks good.

Criteria, written down

Before anything ships

  1. It feels photographed, not generated.
  2. It has grain, texture, life.
  3. You can read the emotion in two seconds.
  4. The composition is a little imperfect, on purpose.
  5. It could not belong to any other brand.

When in doubt: fix. Never ship.

# ICM Kit. Intelligent Context Management for Claude Code

This kit is a folder system designed to work with **Claude Code** in a structured, efficient and scalable way. It works as an "operating system" for your business inside the editor.

---

## What is ICM?

**ICM (Intelligent Context Management)** is a way to organize the context Claude receives in every session. Instead of explaining everything from scratch each time, the system loads the right files for the task at hand.

There are four layers:
- **L0**: `CLAUDE.md` (the main router, always loaded)
- **L1**: `CONTEXT.md` (the load table: what to load and when)
- **L2**: `stages/` and `clients/` (one workspace per project)
- **L3**: `shared/references/` (the persistent knowledge base)

---

### 1. Copy the folder
```bash
cp -r icm-kit/ ~/your-business/
cd ~/your-business/
```

### 2. Fill in the reference files
These are the only files you must complete before you start:

| File | What to fill in |
|---|---|

## Triggers (single word)
| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|

## Model strategy

| Phase | Model | Effort | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🧠 PLANNING (plan mode) | Your strongest model | Max | Project architecture, complex decisions |
| ⚡ EXECUTION | The fast model | High (default) | Copy, prompts, editing files |
| 🔍 QUICK SCAN | Fast mode | Low | Review a brief, scan references |
| 📄 MULTIMODAL | Your strongest model | High | Analyze client PDFs |
| ⬆️ ESCALATION | Your strongest model | Extra | A hard blocker → solve it, then go back to the fast model |

### Protocol (mandatory): always state the model

**At the start of every answer**, show a visible block with the phase and the model it calls for. Announce it again whenever the phase changes:

```
🧠 PLANNING  → strongest model · Effort Max    (plan mode)
⚡ EXECUTION → fast model · Effort High         (implementing)
🔍 SCAN      → fast mode · Effort Low
⬆️ ESCALATE  → strongest model · Effort Extra
```

Rules:
- **Plan mode = strongest model** (plan, decide architecture). **Execution = fast model** (write, edit, produce). Announce the switch when you move from planning to executing.
- The agent **does not switch models on its own**: it says which one applies and **the user changes it in the app's selector**. The block is the reminder.
- If for any reason you are not on the recommended model, say so anyway ("I'm on the fast model, this would be better on the strongest one") so the user can decide.

# Stage 01. Research

> Estimated time: 2 to 4 h

## Input
| Source | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `clients/{client}/brief.md` | The client's initial brief | Project goal, budget, deadline |
| `shared/references/identity.md` | Business identity | Keep voice and positioning |
| `shared/references/voice.md` | Tone + forbidden phrases | Filter language before any output |

## Process
1. Read the client brief (or ask the key questions if it is incomplete)
2. Analyze the client's digital presence: site, social, current communication
3. Identify 3 to 5 relevant references: the ones the client mentioned plus the ones that fit the industry
4. Analyze 1 or 2 direct competitors: what they do, what they lack
5. Identify 1 or 2 aspirational references outside the industry
6. Synthesize: 1 paragraph of context plus a list of key findings

## Suggested tools
- `WebFetch` to analyze the client's and competitors' sites and social
- The `Explore` subagent if references are scattered across the project

```markdown
# Research. {client}
**Date:** {YYYY-MM-DD}

## Company / brand context
{1 paragraph}

## What they are doing well
- {3-5 bullets}

## What they lack / the opportunity
- {3-5 bullets}

## Differential opportunity
{1 paragraph: what nobody in their industry is using}

## Relevant references (3-5)
- {URL or description + why it applies}

## Strategic question for the client
{1 question that forces the client to clarify direction}
```

- Wait for a human review of `output/research.md`.

# Stage 02. Strategy

> Estimated time: 1 to 2 h

## Input
| Source | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `stages/01-research/output/research.md` | Stage 1 output | Context and opportunity |
| `shared/references/identity.md` | Business identity | The concept must align with your brand |
| `shared/references/voice.md` | Tone | Filters concept options |
| `clients/{client}/brief.md` | Original brief | The client's business goal |

## Process
1. Define the **core concept**: ONE idea, one sentence maximum. If it needs more, it is not strong enough.
2. Define the **audience**: real psychographics, not demographics. What they feel, fear, want.
3. Define the **dominant emotion / result**: specific, not generic.
4. Define **formats and scope**: which pieces / deliverables we will produce and why.
5. Define a **measurable business goal**: the concrete action we want from the end customer.

## Output
Write to `output/strategy.md` (one page maximum; if it does not fit, it is overcomplicated):
```markdown
# Strategy. {client}

## Core concept
{1 sentence. One idea that runs through everything.}

## Audience
{Psychographics: desires, fears, contradictions.}

## Result / dominant emotion
{1 specific, measurable result, or 1 specific emotion.}

## Deliverables to produce
- {Deliverable 1: description, format, why}
- {Deliverable 2: ...}

## Measurable business goal
{Which metric / action we want to move.}

## Anti-goal
{What we do NOT want to happen.}
```

## Completion
- Human review. If there is no agreement on the concept, go back to Stage 1.
- **Rule:** concept first. Always. If the idea is weak, do not move forward.
- Next stage: `stages/03-direction/`.

# Stage 03. Direction

> Estimated time: 2 to 3 h

## Input
| Source | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `stages/02-strategy/output/strategy.md` | Signed-off strategy | Concept and deliverables defined |
| `stages/01-research/output/research.md` | The client's references | Inspiration bank |
| `shared/references/identity.md` | Aesthetics and identity | Coherence with the brand |
| `clients/{client}/references/brand.md` | The client's brand | Respect the client's identity |

## Process
1. Translate the strategy into **concrete direction**: how does each deliverable look, sound or feel?
2. Define specific references: style, palette, visual / verbal tone per piece
3. Write a **brief per deliverable**: concrete instructions for production
4. If there are AI assets (images, video, copy): write the prompts or briefs in this step
5. Validate internally before moving to production

## Output
Write to `output/`:
- `output/direction.md`, direction decisions (style, tone, palette, references per piece)
- `output/briefs/`, one brief per deliverable (`.md` or `.json` depending on the tool)

## Direction rules
- One direction per deliverable. If there are two possible options, pick one.
- Be specific: not "modern", but "sans-serif type, lots of white space, no icons".
- The brief must be executable without asking anything else.

- Human review of `output/direction.md`.

# Stage 04. Production

> Estimated time: varies with the number of pieces

## Input
| Source | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `stages/03-direction/output/briefs/` | Direction briefs | The base for execution |
| `shared/references/tool-routing.md` | Tool routing | Decide which tool to use for each piece |
| `clients/{client}/references/brand.md` | The client's brand | Visual / verbal consistency |

## Process
1. Take each direction brief
2. Execute with the right tool (per `tool-routing.md`)
3. **Apply the quality criteria** before selecting (see below)
4. If no variation passes the criteria → adjust the brief, do not generate more variations of the same one
5. Select the best result
6. Post-process if needed

## Quality criteria (selection)
✅ Looks / sounds real and specific to the client
✅ Meets the goal defined in `strategy.md`
✅ Respects the direction in `direction.md`

You are helping me start a second brain in Obsidian. We are inside my vault folder (an empty folder is fine, I will open it in Obsidian afterwards).
